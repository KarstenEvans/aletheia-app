Quality gates before agreeing to buy

1 · Identity & origin

Exact cultivar, country, province, farm or mill, harvest, lot, seed authenticity, trademark/plant-variety permissions. Declare actual origin; jasmine style is not Thai origin. Keep traceability through repacking.

2 · Bran intact

Buy dehusked edible grain, unpolished or minimally milled with retained pigmented bran. Husk/hull is inedible and removed. Verify milling degree, colour uniformity, moisture, broken grain, odour and shelf life. Avoid artificial colouring and red yeast rice substitution.

3 · Contaminants & growing site

Request farm/irrigation source and independent representative lot COA for inorganic arsenic (not just total As), cadmium, lead, pesticides, relevant mycotoxins, and risk-based microbiology. Specify mg/kg, method, detection limits and accredited laboratory scope. Use current destination limits and importer checks; hold or reject failed/undocumented lots. Organic status is not a contaminant certificate.

FSA arsenic guidance · GB import restrictions

4 · Nutritional evidence

Request human GI/insulin data, cooked resistant starch, RDS/SDS and starch structure; measure anthocyanins, C3G, P3G and proanthocyanidins by comparable assays and record cooking retention. Keep study findings separate from permitted pack claims. Rice is naturally gluten-free, including glutinous rice; control cross-contact and verify ≤20 mg/kg if labelled gluten-free.

Gluten-free rules
Regional risk watch: Bengal Delta groundwater (parts of Bangladesh and West Bengal) has documented arsenic risks; GB's 2026/27 monitoring plan lists rice from Thailand and Vietnam for heavy metals. Risk is farm-, water- and cultivar-specific, not a country-wide rejection or approval. Test every supply lot under a risk-based plan. Bengal review · GB monitoring plan

Rice Passport · starch and pigments

Assess the exact cultivar and lot, not the colour alone. Enter only measured results. Blank means unknown. No composite health score or automatic medical claims.

Starch quality

Human GI and insulin index; rapidly and slowly digestible starch; resistant starch after cooking. Amylose and chain-length distribution help explain digestibility but do not certify low GI. RS2 is native granular starch; RS3 forms after cooking and cooling. Wholegrain barriers (RS1) and starch–lipid complexes (RS5) may also matter.

2026 IRRI study · RS subtypes

Pigment quality

Measure total anthocyanins, C3G, P3G and total proanthocyanidins separately; specify analytical standard. Red is usually proanthocyanidin-rich; purple and black are often anthocyanin-rich. Retention after cooking matters. No validated universal therapeutic concentration.
