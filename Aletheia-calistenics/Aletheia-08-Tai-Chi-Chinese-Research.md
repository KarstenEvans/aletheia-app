## Summary
Three different course families should be distinguished:
1. **Traditional standing Tai Chi** (e.g. simplified 24-form, 八法五步) — useful for understanding terminology and form sequencing, but many standing weight shifts and turns cannot be safely replaced by merely sitting down.
2. **Purpose-designed seated/wheelchair Tai Chi** — preferred source for building a chair course; adapts the martial form and biomechanics intentionally.
3. **General seated mobility** (NHS/BHF) — useful and accessible, but not necessarily authentic Tai Chi. Our current `Chair Movement: Calm & Steady` is correctly labelled as gentle mobility / Tai Chi-inspired hand flow, not as a formal Tai Chi teaching programme.

## Established seated systems outside mainland Chinese video sites
- **Wu Style 32-movement seated form**, Tai Chi Chuan Association: https://www.tcca.org.uk/Seated_Form.html . Grandmaster Eddie Wu Kwong Yu devised it for practitioners with limited mobility; page links to a YouTube example and lists movement names. Treat as a reference, not a public-domain course.
- **Hong Kong Poly seated 12-form**, named techniques and small pilot research in https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4320788/ and https://cancerinformation.com.hk/web/2013/08/01/%E5%9D%90%E5%BC%8F%E5%A4%AA%E6%A5%B5%E6%8B%B3%EF%BC%8D%E5%9D%90%E4%BA%AB%E5%85%B6%E6%88%90/ . Promising for sitting balance; results from small cohorts must not be advertised as guaranteed outcomes.
- **Open-access wheelchair Tai Chi ball research:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2022.935986/full . Lists twelve ball-based patterns including opening, cloud hands, crane wing and closing; distinguishes adapted wheelchair Tai Chi from standing form. This is research, not permission to copy the figures/patented apparatus.
- **Warrior Tai Chi YouTube channel:** https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCVg9yKrA5dWxIBagUm9f8mA . Channel existence observed; the user has not yet confirmed this is the remembered channel.

3. Provide linked demonstrations (original sites or creator-permitted embeds) with optional Chinese/English subtitles and a switchable text glossary.
4. Label each source *official instruction*, *research study*, *free creator video*, or *editorial adaptation*, including access/copyright uncertainty.
5. Preserve NHS chair-safety and opt-out constraints; adapt forms based on clinical input, particularly for spinal/shoulder/neurological symptoms.
6. Never embed publisher videos/images as owned Aletheia assets without explicit permission or suitable licence.
7. Avoid the language 'tai chi exercises treat diabetes / cure back pain / rapidly burn fat'. Evidence about benefits is not a guarantee.

### Acceptance criteria added to Aletheia Improve

For any link marketed as **free exercise instruction**, require:
- Direct video/player or clearly listed individual lessons, not a sports-office notice/news index.
- Concrete form names and demonstration/explanation, not just event dates.
- Clear free/paywall status, language, standing/seated/Qigong categorisation and UK availability uncertainty.
- If only an administrative page, place it in **Background research**, not in the main *Start learning* cards.
- Do not confuse metadata verification with watching/clinically evaluating the exercise.
- Source videos and wording must not be downloaded/rehosted without permission.
