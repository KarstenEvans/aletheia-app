## Source-based English lesson: how the instructor teaches Cloud Hands

### 1. Start with a single hand

- Raise the **right hand with the palm turned outwards**.
- Draw a gradual circular path **downwards** while turning the body slightly left.
- As the hand rises again, change the orientation of the palm; allow the **waist to lead the hand towards the right** into a downward pressing motion.
- Do not think of it as an arm-only circle. Repeat and learn the continuous curve.
- Swap to the **left hand** and learn the same circular principle on the other side.
- Keep rotating the palm as the body turns.

**Instructor's emphasis:** learn the feeling of a circle before coordinating two hands.

### 2. Add the second hand: high and low change places

- When turning right, keep the **right hand more outward** and the **left hand closer to the body**; do not overreach by pushing the inner/left hand out.
- Practise a two-level exchange: the **left hand supports upward** while the **right hand presses down**.
- Let the **waist rotate while the hands remain organised in front of the torso**.
- Near the opposite side, **roll the wrists** so the palms briefly face one another.
- Follow with a gentle downward press that coordinates with the body's centre and a naturally lowering exhalation; do **not** force the breath or hold it.
- Return the hands through the waist-led circle and exchange their roles.

**Instructor's emphasis:** `以腰带臂` — the waist leads the arms. Mere arm waving does not train the whole-body method.

### 3. Keep a useful shape in the arms

- **Do not lift the shoulders** when raising the hands: let the shoulders settle.
- Keep the **elbows comfortably relaxed/downward**, not stiffly pushed out.
- Leave a **small open space beneath the armpits**; do not squeeze upper arms tight to the chest.
- Draw **rounded, unbroken arcs** instead of abrupt vertical presses once the method is familiar.

**Instructor's context:** she links this loose arm structure to traditional push/neutralise applications of Tai Chi. That is an explanation of the form's martial origin, not a self-defence technique we need to replicate in the gentle chair lesson.

### 4. Learn the traditional standing footwork separately

- The preceding form finishes in a **bow stance**.
- As weight comes backwards, **turn the right foot slightly in**, ease the heel up and draw it towards the other foot.
- Feel a distinct alternation between **supporting ('full')** and **light ('empty')** feet.
- To travel sideways left, turn the centre right, **place the left toes down first**, then gradually settle the whole foot and transfer weight onto it.
- Return and change to the other side. Keep the foot oriented sensibly: not exaggeratedly turned out or in.
- Keep the body vertically organised; do not thrust the hips backwards on the step. Lower the centre gently when appropriate.

**Important:** these are **the teacher's STANDING Tai Chi steps**. Do not add them to a chair routine, and do not prescribe them for users with balance, nerve, hip, foot or back limitations without appropriate professional assessment.

### 5. Put the hands and feet together

- The preceding form **Parting the Wild Horse's Mane** closes into a rounded **'holding a ball'** arm shape.
- Coordinate the hands' exchange with shifting the body's support and changing steps.
- The **waist directs the hands**; changing direction also changes the hands' palm orientation.
- Let **hands and feet follow one another**, rather than moving in separate isolated bursts.
- Aim for **smooth, uninterrupted, circular movement**.

**Instructor's emphasis:** `手脚相随` (hands and feet coordinate), `连绵不断` (continuous flow), and `划圆` (make circular pathways).

### 6. Teaching drills she demonstrates

**Fingertip-touch drill**
- With one palm high and one low, let the pressing upper palm lightly meet the other hand's fingertips.
- Switch hands and repeat as the waist turns.
- Purpose: help the beginner feel the relative hand position and structured exchange, not to strike the hands forcefully.

**Wall-touch waist-turn drill, standing**
- Stand roughly one foot-length away from a wall (her teaching demonstration).
- Start with a small waist rotation to touch the wall with the **same-side hand**.
- For more challenge, turn enough to touch with the **opposite hand**, or practise gently with a partner.
- In her progression, she lowers into a mild standing-posture exercise and repeats the waist-guided turn.

**Aletheia safety decision:** do not include the wall-touch drill in the chair course: its rotation and reach can be inappropriate for spinal or shoulder problems and for people with balance concerns. A user may study the source video independently.

### 7. Finish with the continuous demonstration

- Combine the circular hands, palm rolls, waist turn, weight shift, and stepping.
- Repeat across directions without sudden halts.
- Wu Man closes the lesson with a narrated full-form demonstration.

## Separately proposed Aletheia chair-course inspiration (**NOT from Wu Man's seated teaching**)

Wu Man teaches a standing form, **not a seated modification**. We should not present the following as instructions she gave:

- Study only the **arm-level ideas** first: one hand circles, palm changes, two hands alternate.
- Sit with sufficient support, feet steady and no forced waist rotation.
- If any arm or shoulder movement is painful or causes new symptoms, reduce range or omit it.
- **Avoid suggesting hip/waist twisting** as a standard seated modification where spinal, hip, shoulder, dizziness or neurological symptoms exist.
- Use a modest range for gentle original Aletheia hand-flow demonstrations; make clear these are *Tai Chi-inspired mobility*, not faithful Wu Man Eight-Form Tai Chi.
- If genuine seated Tai Chi is later taught, obtain a qualified seated Tai Chi teacher's review and an appropriate safety screen.
