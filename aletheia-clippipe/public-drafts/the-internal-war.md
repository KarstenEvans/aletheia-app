# The Internal War: Which ageing cells are worth protecting?

**Aletheia research invitation · Sif's mead-hall analogy**

Imagine a village of ageing Vikings. Some veterans still repair longboats, train apprentices, and keep the village running. Others have barricaded themselves inside the mead hall, smashed the benches, and set the furniture on fire.

The biological research question is not simply *how do we get rid of old cells?*

It is **how does the body distinguish cells that remain useful from persistent senescent cells whose inflammatory secretions can contribute to tissue dysfunction?**

We're interested in several distinct, testable questions:

- How do natural killer cells, macrophages and other immune mechanisms identify and clear certain senescent cells?
- Does exercise alter immune surveillance of persistent senescence, and if so, in which tissues and circumstances?
- What is known about sleep, circadian rhythms, and immune clearance, rather than general claims that sleep “removes senescent cells”?
- How do intermittent senescence and senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) vary by tissue and context?
- Which findings are established in humans, which come from animal studies, and which remain hypotheses?

This is an **open evidence-gathering request**, not a claim that any intervention has been proven to clear senescent cells, and not medical advice.

**Request:** Please share primary studies, negative findings, plausible counterexamples, and precise mechanisms. Especially valuable: studies that distinguish beneficial transient senescence from harmful persistent senescence.

The aim is to protect the Vikings still fixing the longboats, while finding out what to do about the ones setting the hall ablaze.
