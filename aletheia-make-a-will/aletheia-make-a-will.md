# Aletheia Make a Will

[Aletheia Protocol](https://github.com/KarstenEvans/aletheia-protocol) — evidence, provenance, uncertainty and human control.

[Thalia Protocol](https://github.com/KarstenEvans/thalia-protocol/blob/main/THALIA_PROTOCOL.md) — optional appropriate humour and positivity, never a substitute for evidence.

**Version:** 0.1.0 (18 November 2026)  
**Scope:** England and Wales only  
**Status:** educational prototype, not a legally verified will, a regulated advice service, trust deed, or authoritative inheritance-tax calculation.

## Four modules

1. **Make a Will:** a minimal questionnaire leading to a clearly marked review draft for a simple adult-beneficiary case, or a solicitor briefing when complex. Never claim to have signed, witnessed, registered or validated a will.
2. **Family Handover:** private provider and document inventory, household accounts and bill-payment continuity. Keep separate from the actual will, which can become public after probate. No passwords, recovery codes, PINs or full account numbers. Browser never uploads or stores entries; Markdown exports are unencrypted.
3. **DIY Probate:** check registration, original will, bank/mortgage notifications, Tell Us Once, IHT, letters of administration/probate, debt and distribution.
4. **Consider a Family Trust:** compare sole/joint title, lifetime gifting, will trusts, discretionary family trusts, life policies and pensions. Never automatically create a trust deed or promise tax avoidance or care-fee exemption.

## Typical family case

A home worth £400,000 with £150,000 outstanding mortgage and £150,000 savings leaves £400,000 in approximate house equity plus cash, before pensions, other assets, prior gifts and liabilities. This is **not** an estate-tax computation. A parent leaving an eligible home to direct descendants may qualify for basic IHT threshold £325,000 plus residence nil-rate band up to £175,000, subject to conditions. Transferable unused thresholds can matter to spouses/civil partners, not automatically unmarried cohabitants. Most unused pensions and death benefits enter inheritance-tax calculations for deaths on or after **6 April 2027**; review scheme rules, beneficiary nominations, exemptions and any later Budget changes.

## Property and mortgage after death

- **Joint tenants:** title generally passes automatically to surviving owner; HM Land Registry form DJP can remove deceased registered owner. This does **not** extinguish the mortgage charge.
- **Tenants in common:** deceased's beneficial share passes by will/intestacy, potentially via a properly drafted will trust. The surviving registered joint proprietor continues to hold the legal title.
- **Sole owner and deceased-only mortgage:** inheritance does not automatically transfer the mortgage contract or preserve a fixed rate. Ask lender about repayment, product switches, refinancing, protections and reasonable forbearance.
- **Unmarried partner:** cohabitation alone confers neither spouse IHT exemption nor an automatic share of the deceased's estate. Get advice on any beneficial interest, occupation or dependant claim.
- **Lender rate change:** distinguish expiry of an agreed fixed rate from refusal of a like-for-like switch, delay, bereavement or vulnerability handling. FCA MCOB 11.8.1 says a lender should not take advantage of inability to move provider, and FOS examines unfair rate treatment; not all increases are unlawful. Ask for written mortgage offer, rate expiry, rate-change notices, available rates, affordability decision and complaint response.
- **Banks and bills:** deceased's sole account is likely frozen and direct debits can stop. A qualifying joint account normally continues; the survivor should have enough legally accessible own/joint funds for expenses and arrange utility/mortgage direct debits. Do not use deceased logins/cards. Tell Us Once does not notify private banks, mortgage providers and most insurers/pensions.

## Gifting a house or adding children to deeds

- Normally the IHT rule for outright lifetime gifts to individuals is **seven years**, not ten.
- Gifting a house to children and still living there rent-free normally creates a **gift with reservation of benefit**, so it can count in the donor's estate even after ten years.
- Paying genuine open-market rent and an appropriate share of bills may change analysis, but has its own costs. A part-share gift where the new owners genuinely share occupation has a specific exception; professional review is advisable.
- A home transferred while mortgaged usually needs lender consent; assumption of debt may trigger **SDLT in England**, while Wales uses **LTT**. Tax on capital gains, income, divorce, bankruptcy and care assessment also matters.
- There is **no universal seven-year care-fees exemption**. Deliberate deprivation of assets may be challenged under Care Act guidance.
- Adult-child joint ownership is not the same as a family trust and does not guarantee simpler mortgages or lower tax.

## Family trusts for ordinary households

- **Bare trust:** named adult beneficiaries can generally demand their property; not permanent multigenerational control.
- **Will trust / right of occupation:** may allow a survivor to live in the house while eventually benefiting children; requires individually drafted, tax-aware legal terms and consideration of mortgage/title.
- **Discretionary trust:** can benefit children, grandchildren and later descendants. Trustees choose permitted payments, but it is not a tax-free bank. Relevant-property IHT can occur at creation, every 10 years, and on distributions; income tax and administration also apply. New trusts may need registration. England-and-Wales trust perpetuity rules may limit duration (often 125 years).
- **Life insurance in trust:** can provide liquidity for living expenses/mortgage according to policy terms.
- **Pensions/SIPP:** update expression-of-wishes and understand survivor benefits and the 2027 IHT change.
- **Low-cost route:** inspect title and existing life cover, get wills and nominations correct, and set up legitimate bill continuity first. If a complex trust is justified, obtain two written fixed-scope quotes from appropriately regulated and insured specialists, including ongoing fees. Do not use a generic unreviewed deed or a provider promising to shield the house from all IHT and care fees.


## Finding legitimate, lower-cost help with a family trust

Don't select a trust provider based on an advertisement or fixed-price sales pitch alone. For a family home with a mortgage, compare a **single fixed-fee independent will/title/pension review** with a separately priced trust structure; include taxes, trust registration, yearly trust accounts/returns, trustee replacement and winding-up fees.

- [Law Society Find a Solicitor](https://solicitors.lawsociety.org.uk/): search wills, probate and trusts; use it as a directory, **not** a guarantee of quality.
- [Solicitors Regulation Authority register](https://www.sra.org.uk/consumers/register/): independently verify the firm and solicitor are authorised, and check alerts where available.
- [STEP Find a TEP](https://advisingfamilies.org/uk/find-a-tep/): identify estate and trust practitioners with the relevant qualification; check professional status and actual services separately.
- [CMA consumer guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unregulated-legal-services-consumer-protection-law-guidance): compare executor appointments, conflicts of interest, cancellation and added charges.

An advertised low-cost will does **not** require appointing the writing firm as professional executor. Ask for the option to appoint your own trusted adult executors and to hire professionals only for tasks that need them. Having a regulated professional draw a complex trust is safer than pretending a generic family-trust deed is reliable.

## Lawful gifts

£3,000 annual exemption (unused portion can carry forward one tax year); individual small gifts up to £250 if not mixed with another exemption for that recipient; qualifying normal gifts out of regular surplus income may be immediately exempt if the giver maintains their normal standard of living. Keep written dates, amounts, recipient and financial evidence. Taper relief is often misunderstood: it does not discount the estate's whole inheritance tax charge.

## After a death: practical order

1. Register death in England and Wales normally within **five days after the medical examiner confirms** it can be registered, and secure the house, keys, insurance, pets and original will.
2. Identify executor, surviving legal owners, mortgage, next payment and lawfully accessible emergency funds.
3. Use Tell Us Once (reference normally within 28 days); separately inform banks, mortgage providers, pensions, insurer, employer and utilities.
4. Gather statements, property title, debt balances, life insurance, pensions, recent gifts and any previous wills.
5. Value estate and check inheritance-tax reporting and payment. Ask bank about direct payment of funeral bills if appropriate.
6. Check whether grant of probate/administration is actually needed; follow GOV.UK if DIY.
7. Pay proper debts and taxes, keep accounts, and distribute only when legally safe.

## Complexity stop gates

No auto sign-ready will if: outside England and Wales, under 18, coercion/capacity concerns, minor or vulnerable dependants, unmarried partner, blended/disputed family, unclear guardianship, foreign property, business/farm, existing trusts, proposed will trust, life interest or property-title dispute. Produce an educational briefing for a professional instead.

An England-and-Wales will generally needs correct signed written execution in the presence of **two independent adult witnesses present together**, who then sign in the maker's presence. Witnesses and their spouses/civil partners should not receive gifts. Keep the original signed will secure and findable. A digital worksheet is not a registered or executed will.
