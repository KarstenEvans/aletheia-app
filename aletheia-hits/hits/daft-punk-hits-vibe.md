## Evidence
Daft Punk's Thomas Bangalter describes *Around the World* as analogue synth bass, drum machines and talk-box vocals, and later vocoder processing as an essential creative 'robot voice' concept. Their music makes a few recurring rhythmic elements feel fresh through rearrangement and changing timbres. [Wax Poetics interview](https://magazine.waxpoetics.com/article/daft-punk-go-from-sampling-disco-records-to-creating-a-dance-masterpiece/). Vocoder, talk box and pitch correction are different technologies; do not treat them as one filter. [Bjango technical analysis](https://bjango.com/articles/daftpunkvocaleffects/).

## Original transferable HitsVibe
- Strong dance groove with syncopated funk/disco-informed bass, purposeful rests and re-voiced rhythmic layers.
- **Muting is composition:** create several independent cells, then combine them differently every 8–16 bars. Change one part without losing the central groove.
- **Processed words as instruments:** fragment, gate, vocode, formant-shift or repeat only the intentionally synthetic backing character; leave an intimate narrator clear and unprocessed.
- **Human and machine:** sometimes precise and quantised, sometimes lightly swung, creating constructive tension.

## I'm Leaving example
The irritating phrase “IT policy... social media” is cut and rhythmically interlocked with the percussion. Beneath it, a restrained spoken voice stays clear. After the final exchange, the bass comes back broader and warmer.
