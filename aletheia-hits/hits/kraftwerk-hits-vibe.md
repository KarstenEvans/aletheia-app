# Kraftwerk HitsVibe

[Aletheia Protocol](https://github.com/KarstenEvans/aletheia-protocol) (evidence, provenance, uncertainty) | [Thalia Protocol](https://github.com/KarstenEvans/thalia-protocol/blob/main/THALIA_PROTOCOL.md) (optional considerate humour)

## Evidence
Kraftwerk developed minimalist electronic arrangements built from sequences, electronic percussion and treated voices. Ralf Hütter described “sound poetry”: choosing words for both sonic form and semantic meaning, including voice as an instrument. [1976 interview](https://insheepsclothinghifi.com/kraftwerk-1976-interview/). Their work often explores the interplay between human and machine through technology and repetition; this is interpretive rather than a claim about every track.

## Original transferable HitsVibe
- **Precision with microscopic difference:** one clear pulse, two or three interlocking sequencer patterns, each carrying rests and accents, a removed hit or altered note when a phrase returns.
- **Synthetic language:** spoken syllables can have meaning while also functioning as pitched or percussive material. Short words are fertile for gating, repeats, vocoder-like effects and sharply placed silence.
- **Arrangement:** sparse/dry drums against rare spacious synth intervals. Filter motion, automation and tiny pattern shifts create expectation without an overload of notes.
- **Contrast:** a robot voice can serve a conceptual character, but an intimate human narrator belongs on a separate, less processed channel.

## I'm Leaving example
The meeting refrain repeats in machine precision, with incremental rhythmic variations each time. The system cannot stop 'summarising'. Eventually the narrator's words puncture the pattern, the machine suddenly disappears and a warmer musical space opens.

## Boundaries
No use of copyrighted Kraftwerk arrangements, masters, lyrics or unique robotic vocal imitation. Study sound design, invent new music.
