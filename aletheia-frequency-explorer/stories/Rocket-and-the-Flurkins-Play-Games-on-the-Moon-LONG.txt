Rocket and the Flurkins Play Games on the Moon
A short story for young readers

Rocket was not a rocket.

Rocket was a cat.

He was orange, fluffy, and extremely sure that every chair in the house belonged to him.

One evening, Rocket was sitting on the windowsill, looking at the moon.

“I bet they have excellent chasing games up there,” he said.

“On the moon?” asked a cheerful voice.

Rocket turned around so fast he nearly fell off the sill.

Floating outside the window in a tiny silver flying saucer was his alien friend, Bloop.

Bloop had three eyes, two socks that did not match, and one very happy smile.

“Quite right,” said Rocket, trying to look as if he met flying-saucer aliens every Tuesday. “I was just thinking about a moon visit.”

“Perfect!” cried Bloop. “I came to ask exactly that. The Flurkins are having games night.”

“Flurkins?” asked Rocket.

“Moon cats,” said Bloop. “Fluffy, bouncy, and very keen on snacks.”

“Excellent,” said Rocket. “I also believe strongly in snacks.”

So Rocket leapt through the open window and into the saucer.

WHOOOOSH!

Off they flew.

The stars glittered. The Earth looked like a blue marble. Rocket pressed his paws against the window and gasped.

“The moon is bigger up close,” he said.

“That is generally how close things work,” said Bloop.

Rocket nodded wisely. “Yes. I knew that.”

Soon the saucer landed with a tiny boing on the moon.

Waiting there were five Flurkins.

They had bright eyes, long curly tails, and soft glowing whiskers that shone like moonbeams. One was silver. One was blue. One was purple with polka dots. One was fluffy like a dandelion. The last one wore a tiny gold cape for no reason at all.

“Welcome, Rocket!” they cheered.

“I am Captain Mizzle,” said the Flurkin in the cape. “These are Pippa, Zoomlet, Nib, and Floof.”

Rocket gave his best polite cat nod, which meant, Hello, I am pleased to meet you and I may permit friendship.

“What games are we playing?” he asked.

“First,” said Captain Mizzle, “Moon-Bounce Chase!”

The Flurkins sprang into the air.

Boing!
Boing!
Boing!

On the moon, everyone bounced much higher than usual.

Rocket took one practice jump.

BOOOING!

He floated upward, spun around, and landed in a soft puff of silver moon dust.

“Again!” he shouted.

So they chased each other over little craters and around shiny rocks, bouncing so high they looked like popcorn.

Rocket almost tagged Zoomlet, but Zoomlet bounced over his head.

Pippa nearly caught Rocket’s tail, but Rocket made a daring sideways pounce.

Bloop blew a tiny whistle and shouted, “No biting the referee!”

“I wasn't biting,” said Rocket. “I was investigating.”

Next they played Crater Hide-and-Seek.

Nib hid behind a round rock.
Floof hid in a crater.
Captain Mizzle hid behind Bloop, which was not a good hiding place because Bloop kept giggling.

Rocket found them all.

Then it was time for Stringball.

Bloop pulled out a shiny silver string with a floating moon ball tied to the end.

Every cat — Earth cat and moon cat alike — froze and stared.

The ball wiggled.

Six pairs of paws launched at once.

POUNCE!

There was tumbling.

There was bouncing.

There was one very surprised somersault from Captain Mizzle.

At one point Rocket and Floof both grabbed the string and floated slowly in a circle, looking very proud of themselves.

“Best game ever,” whispered Rocket.

After the games, everyone sat together on a wide flat rock and shared moon snacks.

The Flurkins had star-shaped biscuits that fizzed softly on your tongue.

Rocket brought crunchy fish treats from Earth.

“These are amazing!” said Pippa.

“Thank you,” said Rocket, puffing out his chest.

They looked up at the sky. Earth glowed blue and white in the distance.

“Do you ever feel lonely up here?” Rocket asked.

Captain Mizzle shook his fluffy head.

“Not when we have friends,” he said. “And not when someone remembers to bring snacks.”

“That helps,” Rocket agreed.

Then Bloop checked his watch.

“Oh dear. If we don’t leave now, your humans will think you’ve run away to become an astronaut.”

Rocket stood up. “I have had a wonderful evening. Your bouncing is excellent. Your games are excellent. Your snacks are strange, but excellent.”

“Come back soon!” cried the Flurkins.

“Bring more fish treats!” shouted Zoomlet.

“And the stringball!” added Floof.

Rocket climbed into the saucer beside Bloop.

As they lifted off, Rocket looked back and saw the Flurkins waving their glowing whiskers.

When he arrived home, he jumped back onto the windowsill just as the moon shone through the glass.

He sat down neatly and washed one paw, because that is what cats do after extraordinary adventures.

His human walked past and said, “There you are, Rocket. Have you had an exciting evening?”

Rocket gave a calm little meow.

He did not mention moon-bounce chase.
He did not mention flying saucers.
He did not mention fizzy star biscuits.

Some things are hard for humans to understand.

But later, when he looked out at the moon, he smiled.

Far away, five tiny whiskers twinkled back.

And on the windowsill beside him sat a silver piece of string with a floating moon ball tied to the end.

Rocket pounced.

The End
