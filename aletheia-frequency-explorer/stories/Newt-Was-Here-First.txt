Newt Was Here First

Before the shed, there was the pond.

Before the flowerpots, there was the pond.

Before the garden chair that wobbled because somebody had put one leg on a paving stone and then pretended not to notice, there was the pond.

And before almost everything else, there was Newt.

Newt was a newt.

This saved a great deal of explanation.

He lived beneath a flat stone at the edge of the water and considered himself Senior Management.

Nobody had appointed him.

Nobody had been asked.

This is how Senior Management often begins.

Every morning Newt inspected the pond.

Duckweed: present.

Water: wet.

Snail on the wrong leaf: still on the wrong leaf.

Frog: suspicious.

Newt nodded.

Excellent.

Then, one Tuesday, a goddess arrived.

She did not arrive in a thunderbolt.

She did not descend on a golden cloud.

She came through the side gate wearing muddy boots and saying, “I am completely lost.”

Newt raised his head above the water.

The goddess looked at the pond.

The pond looked back in the way ponds do, which is to say not very much.

“Hello,” she said.

Newt said nothing.

“I’m Tiff.”

Newt continued saying nothing.

“I’m a goddess.”

Newt blinked.

Tiff frowned.

“You could look slightly more impressed.”

Newt climbed onto his stone.

“I was here first,” he said.

Tiff looked around.

“At the pond?”

“At everything.”

“The house?”

Newt considered this.

“Most things.”

“The planet?”

“Do not make this difficult.”

Tiff sat beside the pond.

Her boots were covered in mud, her hair had escaped in several directions, and one tiny silver star was stuck to her sleeve.

Newt stared at it.

Tiff picked it off.

“Occupational hazard.”

“What do goddesses do?” asked Newt.

“Depends on the goddess.”

“What do you do?”

Tiff thought.

“I wander.”

“That is not a job.”

“It is if you are very good at it.”

Newt did not approve.

The pond had systems.

The pond had procedures.

The pond had a snail who was STILL on the wrong leaf.

Wandering sounded dangerously unstructured.

Then Tiff dipped one finger into the water.

A ring of light travelled across the pond.

Every duckweed leaf glittered.

The frog sneezed.

Three bubbles rose from the mud and popped in perfect time.

Newt stared.

“What was that?”

“Sorry,” said Tiff. “Sometimes the goddess bit leaks.”

“Can you turn it off?”

“Usually.”

“Usually?”

“Almost usually.”

Newt climbed down from his stone.

“This pond does not need magic.”

“I didn’t say it did.”

“It has been perfectly functional for years.”

“I can see that.”

“The water is wet.”

“Very impressive.”

“The duckweed is present.”

“Remarkable.”

“And that snail is under investigation.”

The snail moved half a centimetre.

Newt pointed.

“Aha!”

Tiff smiled.

Then the sky darkened.

Rain began.

Not polite rain.

Not little drops.

This was the sort of rain that arrives sideways and attempts to remove the garden.

Tiff stood up.

Newt did not.

Newts are not famous for umbrellas.

Water rushed from the path towards the pond.

Leaves spun into it.

A flowerpot toppled.

The wobbly chair finally admitted it had a problem.

Newt swam hard.

The pond was rising.

A tiny youngster clung to a reed.

Newt pushed through the water towards it, but the current caught him and turned him around.

Tiff saw.

She did not shout a spell.

She did not turn the rain into diamonds.

She put both hands into the muddy water.

“Easy,” she whispered.

The rushing stream from the path bent.

Just a little.

Enough.

It curled away from the nest of reeds and poured harmlessly across the lawn.

Newt reached the youngster.

Together they tucked beneath the flat stone.

A few minutes later the rain softened.

Then stopped.

The pond settled.

The duckweed returned to the important business of floating about.

The frog looked offended, which was normal.

Newt climbed onto his stone.

Tiff sat beside him.

Neither spoke for a while.

Eventually Newt said, “You moved the water.”

“A little.”

“You could have changed the whole pond.”

“Yes.”

“But you didn’t.”

“No.”

“Why?”

Tiff shrugged.

“It was your pond.”

Newt stared at her.

Nobody had ever said that before.

People trimmed things.

Moved stones.

Scooped leaves.

Put ornaments in places nobody had requested ornaments.

Once, somebody had introduced a plastic duck.

The pond still discussed it.

But Tiff had power enough to change everything and had chosen only to help where help was needed.

Newt shuffled sideways on his stone.

There was now a small space beside him.

Tiff noticed.

“Is that for me?”

“It might be.”

She sat down.

“You can visit,” said Newt.

“Thank you.”

“But there are rules.”

“Of course there are.”

“Do not rearrange the stones.”

“Agreed.”

“Do not sparkle the duckweed without permission.”

“Fair.”

“And the snail situation is confidential.”

Tiff nodded gravely.

The snail moved another half centimetre.

Both of them pretended not to see.

From then on, Tiff wandered through the garden whenever her journeys brought her near.

Sometimes she brought stories.

Sometimes she brought stars accidentally stuck to her clothes.

Sometimes she brought absolutely nothing and just sat beside the pond.

Newt preferred those visits most.

Because a pond does not always need improving.

A newt does not always need rescuing.

And a goddess does not always need worshipping.

Sometimes you simply need someone who understands that you were there first...

and asks before moving the stones.
