01. The good things that cost very little

A few ideas to try before buying anything. Choose safer battery-operated lanterns for children, and supervise cutting, cooking and costume materials.

🪔

Turnip lantern challenge

The predecessor of the familiar pumpkin lantern has British and Irish roots. Draw a face first, ask an adult to prepare the turnip, and light it with a battery LED rather than an exposed flame.

🍪

Ghost stories at home

Choose one existing book or library story, dim the lights, and take turns adding one impossible detail to a shared original story. No AI-generated “true haunted house” claim needed.

02. How did Halloween get here?

All Hallows’ Eve

The name is tied to the eve of All Saints’ Day on 1 November. All Souls’ Day is on 2 November. Its relationship with older seasonal traditions is more complex than a simple one-origin story.

English Heritage historical discussion ↗

Souling and guising

Door-to-door souling, seasonal food and dressing up have historical connections to modern practices. Modern trick-or-treating was popularised in the United States and later reintroduced in its familiar form in Britain.

Read about souling ↗

Turnips became pumpkins

Lantern customs crossed the Atlantic and adapted to pumpkins, which were easier to carve; the familiar pumpkin lantern later became widespread in Britain too.
