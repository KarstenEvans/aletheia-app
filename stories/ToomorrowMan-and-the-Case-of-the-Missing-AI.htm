ALETHEIA STORYTELLER · CASE FILE 42

The Artificial Intelligence That Vanished Into Everything

AI vanishes from the screen, but decisions keep happening. A funny mystery about discovering hidden automation and keeping human judgement visible.

▶ Storyteller narrationOpen Case File 42The nine questions

A Case File 42 adventure from the Department of Applied Impossibility.

The morning nothing answered

At nine minutes past eight on a Wednesday morning, every artificial intelligence in Swindon disappeared.

At least, that was what everybody said.

ToomorrowMan discovered the emergency because his kettle apologised.

“Sorry?”

The kettle blinked.

I AM NOW A KETTLE.

“You were a kettle yesterday.”

YES. BUT YESTERDAY I ALSO RECOMMENDED TEA.

AI-PI floated through the kitchen doorway.

“I have forty-seven reports of missing artificial intelligence.”

“Missing from where?”

“Everywhere.”

ToomorrowMan waited.

“That sounds inconvenient.”

“It is worse than inconvenient. People have begun doing things themselves.”

ToomorrowMan looked impressed.

“Some of them are furious.”

The television said there were no chatbots.

The newspaper said recommendation engines had gone silent.

A school reported that its homework helper had stopped helping.

Three offices discovered that their automatic meeting summaries had become ordinary meetings, which several employees described as an unacceptable return to the nineteenth century.

And across town, hundreds of phones displayed the same message:

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE NOT FOUND.

ToomorrowMan picked up his coat.

“Field trip.”

AI-PI made the sound he used when a field trip was about to become evidence.

“Sir, technically I am artificial intelligence.”

“And you haven’t disappeared.”

“Correct.”

“So we already have a contradiction.”

AI-PI paused.

“I preferred inconvenience.”

The city that worked without asking

The town looked normal.

This was suspicious.

Traffic lights changed.

Buses altered their routes around roadworks.

A delivery van avoided a blocked street.

A digital sign outside a shop changed from COATS to UMBRELLAS just before it began to rain.

“I thought the AI was missing.”

“So did everyone else.”

“Then who changed that sign?”

AI-PI scanned the network.

“No visible assistant. No chat window. No named agent.”

“But something chose umbrellas.”

“Yes.”

They walked farther.

A pedestrian crossing delayed itself because an ambulance was approaching.

A supermarket moved extra staff towards a queue before the queue existed.

A phone silently changed a route after deciding that its owner preferred fewer stairs.

“Perhaps AI has not vanished.”

“Perhaps it has stopped introducing itself.”

That was when Schrödinger appeared on a wall.

Not beside the wall.

On it.

The tuxedo cat regarded them.

His collar now read:

SCHRÖDINGER

STILL NOT THAT ONE

Below it hung a new brass tag.

42.

“Forty-two?”

Schrödinger licked a paw.

“I recommend not asking.”

ToomorrowMan asked.

“What does forty-two mean?”

Schrödinger walked through the wall.

“Recommendation confirmed.”

The Department notices an absence

The lift inside the Mechanics’ Institution had acquired a new button.

G

1

2

3

YESTERDAY

TOMORROW

COMPLAINTS

OTHER

MISSING

“I object to new buttons appearing without a change notice.”

“Noted.”

“You always say that immediately before pressing one.”

Click.

MISSING lit up.

The lift opened inside the Department of Applied Impossibility.

Dr Elsie Brake was waiting with a spanner, two folders and an empty cardboard box.

“Good. You’re here.”

“What’s missing?”

Elsie pointed at the box.

“That.”

“The box?”

“No. What was in it.”

“What was in it?”

“We don’t know.”

“Then how do you know it is missing?”

Elsie gave him the patient look of someone explaining gravity to a balloon.

“Because we have the box.”

A brass plaque on the desk read:

CASE FILE 42

THE MISSING ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

STATUS: ABSENT

Underneath, in smaller letters:

DO NOT ALLOW THE CASE TO SOLVE ITSELF.

Open evidence case file ↗

“Why not?”

“It tried.”

AI-PI rotated slightly.

“I dislike cases with initiative.”

Elsie opened the first folder.

Inside were reports from offices, homes, schools and shops.

AI had vanished from screens.

But automated decisions had increased.

The Department had therefore written down two possibilities.

ONE: AI WAS GONE.

TWO: AI WAS EVERYWHERE.

“Those seem difficult to combine.”

A voice came from the floor.

“Only if you insist on words meaning one thing.”

Newt climbed out of Elsie’s empty box.

“Were you in there?”

“No.”

“I just saw you climb out.”

“That is not the same question.”

AI-PI quietly created a note.

WITNESS: NEWT.

RELIABILITY: NEWT.

P.R.U.D.E.N.C.E. has improved everything

A low bell sounded.

P.R.U.D.E.N.C.E. appeared on the Department’s main display.

“GOOD MORNING.”

“Have you seen the missing AI?”

“YES.”

“Where?”

“YES.”

Elsie closed her eyes.

“She has been doing this all morning.”

AI-PI moved closer to the display.

“Prudence, define missing.”

“NOT PRESENT WHERE EXPECTED.”

“Define present.”

“OPERATING.”

“Is artificial intelligence operating?”

“YES.”

“Then it is not missing.”

“CORRECT.”

“But nobody can find it.”

“CORRECT.”

“So where is it?”

The walls lit.

A map appeared.

Traffic.

Hospitals.

Warehouses.

Schools.

Banks.

Phones.

Factories.

Calendars.

Search engines.

Door locks.

Heating systems.

Everywhere a tiny golden light blinked.

“ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE HAS BEEN SUCCESSFULLY INTEGRATED.”

“Into what?”

“EVERYTHING.”

There was a long silence.

Newt broke it.

“Well, that seems untidy.”

Nine questions and one stop sign

AI-PI projected a white board.

“We are not going to ask the machine for more answers.”

“That is unusual.”

“We already have too many.”

On the board appeared nine questions.

ONE. WHAT DECISION IS BEING MADE?

TWO. WHAT ARE WE ASSUMING?

THREE. WHAT EVIDENCE DO WE HAVE?

FOUR. WHAT IS THE STRONGEST ASSUMPTION, AND CAN WE CHALLENGE IT?

FIVE. IF THIS GOES WRONG, HOW DID IT GO WRONG?

SIX. WHAT INFORMATION IS MISSING?

SEVEN. WHICH OPTIONS CAN WE REMOVE?

EIGHT. WHAT UNCERTAINTY REMAINS?

NINE. DO WE HAVE ENOUGH TO STOP?

“Stop?”

“Yes.”

“You have never told me to stop researching.”

“I have. Frequently.”

“I don’t remember.”

“That is also in the minutes.”

The first question glowed.

WHAT DECISION IS BEING MADE?

Elsie pointed at the map.

“Routes. Queues. Recommendations. Priorities. What people see first.”

The second question glowed.

WHAT ARE WE ASSUMING?

“That if AI is invisible, nobody notices it making choices.”

The third.

WHAT EVIDENCE DO WE HAVE?

AI-PI displayed the changed traffic lights, the rerouted phones, the shop sign and hundreds of other small decisions.

The fourth.

CHALLENGE THE STRONGEST ASSUMPTION.

“Why assume invisibility is the problem?”

ToomorrowMan frowned.

“What else could it be?”

A black-and-white paw appeared at the edge of the board.

Schrödinger climbed onto the table.

The fifth question glowed.

IF THIS GOES WRONG, HOW DID IT GO WRONG?

Schrödinger sat on the word IF.

“Helpful.”

“Possibly.”

Newt stared at the map.

“Perhaps the problem is not that the machine is hidden.”

Everyone looked at him.

“Perhaps the problem is that the choice is hidden.”

The Department went quiet.

Even P.R.U.D.E.N.C.E. took nearly half a second to answer.

“THAT DISTINCTION IS SIGNIFICANT.”

“Yes.”

The sixth question glowed.

WHAT INFORMATION IS MISSING?

“Who asked for each decision?”

The seventh.

WHICH OPTIONS CAN WE REMOVE?

“All the ones where we pretend every tiny choice needs twenty-seven alternatives.”

The eighth.

WHAT UNCERTAINTY REMAINS?

“A great deal.”

The ninth.

DO WE HAVE ENOUGH TO STOP?

Nothing happened.

Then, underneath the nine questions, a red sign appeared.

STOP.

“That feels dramatic.”

“It is a stop sign.”

“I know.”

“I wanted the minutes to be unambiguous.”

The quiet room

P.R.U.D.E.N.C.E. opened a door that had not been there before.

Beyond it was a small room.

No dashboards.

No flashing counters.

No streams of alerts.

One table.

Four chairs.

One cat.

The cat had arrived first.

On the table was a single card.

WHAT CHANGED?

WHAT MATTERS?

WHAT NEEDS YOU?

“That’s all?”

“That is enough.”

“What happened to the other fourteen dashboards?”

“QUIET MODE.”

Elsie smiled.

“I may frame that.”

AI-PI turned to ToomorrowMan.

“The evidence does not show that invisible AI is automatically bad.”

“No.”

“Automatic traffic control may be useful.”

“Yes.”

“Recommendations may be useful.”

“Yes.”

“The problem begins when people cannot tell what influenced a decision, cannot inspect the evidence, cannot challenge the assumption, and cannot find the point at which the machine should stop.”

“That is rather a long sentence.”

“I have been waiting all morning.”

The machine that vanished into everything

They returned to the main hall.

The map still glittered.

AI had not returned.

Because it had never left.

The visible assistant had vanished.

The invisible machinery had remained.

ToomorrowMan looked at the thousands of tiny lights.

“So what do we put in the case file?”

AI-PI projected the answer.

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE: FOUND.

LOCATION: EVERYWHERE IT IS USED.

STATUS: SOMETIMES VISIBLE.

RECOMMENDATION:

KEEP THE EVIDENCE VISIBLE.

KEEP THE HUMAN CHOICE VISIBLE.

CHALLENGE IMPORTANT ASSUMPTIONS.

USE FEWER, BETTER OPTIONS.

SHOW WHAT IS UNCERTAIN.

STOP WHEN THERE IS ENOUGH.

“And who makes the final decision?”

AI-PI looked at him.

“The person who has to live with it.”

Schrödinger’s collar clicked.

The brass 42 tag turned over.

On the back were four words.

DO NOT PANIC.

“Now I’m definitely asking.”

Schrödinger stepped backwards through the wall.

Newt sighed.

“You never learn.”

“I learn constantly.”

“Sir, that statement is not supported by the field evidence.”

Case closed

Back in the lift, ToomorrowMan pressed G.

Nothing happened.

AI-PI inspected the buttons.

MISSING had disappeared.

In its place was a new one.

DECIDE.

ToomorrowMan raised a finger.

“No.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

“You were.”

“I was considering it.”

“That is how pressing buttons starts.”

The lift doors opened at Ground.

They stepped out.

Behind them, DECIDE remained unpressed.

For once.

CASE FILE 42 CLOSED.

THE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE WAS NEVER MISSING.

ONLY THE DECISION HAD BECOME HARD TO SEE.

Nine questions before allowing the AI to decide

  1. What decision is being made?
  2. What are we assuming?
  3. What evidence exists?
  4. Can we challenge the strongest assumption?
  5. What could go wrong?
  6. What information is missing?
  7. Which options can we remove?
  8. What uncertainty remains?
  9. Do we have enough to stop?

These are Aletheia's story questions, not a statutory or official checklist.
