# ToomorrowMan and the Job That Wasn't There

ToomorrowMan's new neighbour, Mira, had applied for thirty-seven jobs before breakfast. She had received an interview invitation from a company that appeared to sell invisible office chairs.

[voice:TM]
"Perhaps that's the remote-working division," he said.

The advert had a generous salary, a prestigious-sounding employer, and one problem: nobody could find the vacancy on the employer's official careers website.

[voice:AI-PI]
"Sir, a search result proves only that someone wrote a search result. It does not prove that anyone is hiring."

Mira opened Aletheia Job Search, searched the employer's own site, and looked for the exact role, location and application link. She found an old job-board copy and a broken application button.

She marked the vacancy UNVERIFIED, rather than declaring fraud. The difference mattered.

[voice:TM]
"And the seven jobs that ask for a deposit before an interview?"

AI-PI's display flashed amber.

[voice:AI-PI]
"Those merit investigation and extreme caution. Real vacancies do not become real because their graphics are convincing."

Mira tried something else. She asked for a CV check against a genuine engineering coordinator position. Aletheia compared the advert's essential criteria with her actual experience.

It found that Mira's CV said "helped with projects" where the evidence showed she had maintained design records, resolved version conflicts and checked drawings. The fix was not invention. It was precision.

Then Mira called GO WALKABOUT.

[voice:PRUDENCE]
"One bounded excursion," replied P.R.U.D.E.N.C.E. "No infinite lists. No imaginary employers."

They explored adjacent roles: document controller, technical coordinator and information manager. Two ideas did not fit. One opened a useful path for further research.

That afternoon Mira practised an interview question using Situation, Task, Action and Result. Her first answer was impressively long and magnificently vague. Her second had a problem, a decision, an action and a measurable outcome she could actually defend.

[voice:TM]
"So the machine did not find a secret door into employment."

[voice:AI-PI]
"No, sir. It removed a few misleading doors, found a door worth knocking on, and helped Mira explain why she belonged on the other side."

Mira applied herself. The AI did not press Send.

And for once, her list was shorter when she finished than when she started.

# Case note: What the story teaches

1. Verify the **individual live vacancy**, not just a snippet or recruiter homepage.
2. Suspicious recruitment signals warrant checks; an unverified advert alone does not establish a scam.
3. An ATS-friendly CV is readable, relevant and truthful; it is not a collection of invented keywords.
4. Adjacent job titles can widen a search, but new leads need verification.
5. Practise STAR answers with genuine examples, and approve every application yourself.

This is fiction illustrating Aletheia Job Search methods, not a report of a real applicant or actual vacancy.
