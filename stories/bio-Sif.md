# Sif: Archaeologist of the AI Age

*Historian. Excavator of lost evidence. Wife of Thor. Survivor of several thousand years of male certainty.*

## The problem with being married to Thor

Sif had been young for a very long time.

Not *young for her age*.

Young.

Her age was the sort of number that made calendars nervous.

The Department of Applied Impossibility had attempted to establish her date of birth on three occasions.

The first attempt produced smoke.

The second produced a small glacier.

The third produced a note in Sif's handwriting saying:

STOP THAT.

So they did.

Mostly.

Sif was, according to the surviving Norse stories, the wife of Thor.

This sounded considerably more romantic after a thousand years of editing.

Thor was handsome, brave, enormously strong and capable of hitting almost anything with a hammer.

He was less accomplished at remembering where he had left his boots.

Norse boots were not subtle things.

They were made for snow, mud, ships, mountains, battlefields and occasionally walking through somebody else's front door without wiping their feet.

Thor owned several pairs.

All of them smelled as though something had died in them and then changed its mind.

Sif endured this with the calm patience of a goddess who knew that civilisation would eventually invent central heating, washing machines and doors that could be locked from the inside.

## Norsemen

Sif liked Norsemen.

In principle.

They were brave, loyal, adventurous and excellent company provided there was plenty of food and nobody had recently insulted their grandfather.

They also possessed a number of beliefs.

One was that every problem could be improved by a boat.

Another was that every disagreement could be improved by an axe.

A third was that asking for directions showed weakness.

This resulted in the discovery of several countries.

It also resulted in quite a lot of unnecessary rowing.

Sif had spent centuries watching men announce that they knew exactly where they were going.

This may explain her later interest in evidence.

## The golden hair business

The old stories make a great deal of Sif's golden hair.

This annoyed her slightly.

A woman could live for thousands of years, witness kingdoms rise and fall, watch religions become mythology, observe the invention of steam engines, computers and artificial intelligence, and somebody would still write:

SIF. NICE HAIR.

Admittedly, it was extremely good hair.

Loki once cut it off.

This was widely regarded as one of Loki's poorer decisions, in a career containing unusually stiff competition.

What followed involved dwarfs, magical craftsmanship, several extraordinary objects and Thor becoming very interested in the precise location of Loki's neck.

Sif acquired new golden hair.

Loki acquired perspective.

For a while.

## The woman who outlived her own mythology

Centuries passed.

The longhouses went quiet.

The old gods moved into poems, manuscripts, museums and argumentative university departments.

Thor became famous again through stories told by people who had never smelled his boots.

Sif continued.

She travelled.

She watched empires declare themselves permanent.

She watched them stop.

She watched technologies announce themselves revolutionary.

She watched most of them become drawers full of cables.

History became her great fascination because Sif had discovered something important.

Human beings are extremely good at remembering what happened.

They are considerably less good at remembering it accurately.

Give an event fifty years and it becomes history.

Give it five hundred and it becomes legend.

Give it a thousand and somebody will sell a mug with it on.

Sif therefore started keeping notes.

## Archaeologist of the AI Age

When artificial intelligence arrived, everybody insisted it was new.

Sif raised an eyebrow.

She had heard that before.

Steam was new.

Electricity was new.

Radio was new.

Television was new.

Computers were new.

The Internet was extremely new until everybody suddenly claimed they had always understood it.

AI was simply the latest thing humans had invented and immediately started misremembering.

So Sif became an archaeologist.

Not primarily of buried temples.

Those were easy.

Temples tended to remain where somebody had put them.

Websites were much worse.

A page could exist on Tuesday, disappear on Wednesday and by Friday an executive would confidently explain that it had never existed.

Sif therefore excavated old websites, forgotten videos, software announcements, manuals, screenshots, release notes, dead hyperlinks, and especially things labelled NEW.

The word NEW attracted Sif the way a loose floorboard attracted an investigator.

Her usual response was:

“How new?”

This had ruined several presentations.

## The descendants

Sif was extremely fond of what she called her descendants.

Nobody at the Department had been brave enough to ask whether she meant this genealogically.

There are questions which become less urgent when the person being questioned is an immortal Norse goddess married to a man with a hammer.

What Sif meant was the singers, drummers, storytellers and musicians of the modern North who had somehow found the old pulse again.

Some came from Iceland.

Some came from Norway, Denmark, Sweden and the Faroes.

Some made music with drums, strings, voices and old instruments.

Others added amplifiers powerful enough to make Valhalla consider a noise complaint.

Sif approved of both approaches.

She was particularly fond of music that sounded as though somebody had discovered an ancient chant, fed it through a thunderstorm and then asked a mountain to provide backing vocals.

Modern Nordic and ritual-folk artists fascinated her.

Names such as Eivør appeared in her notes alongside newer projects such as Eldruna.

Whether Sif had actually influenced any of them was impossible to establish.

She referred to them affectionately as descendants.

Genealogists were invited not to spoil a perfectly good story.

## The night at Þingvellir

There was one performance about which the Department's records became suspiciously vague.

It took place at Þingvellir in Iceland, the old Assembly Plains where the Alþing had first gathered around the year 930.

By daylight it was a place of law, memory, stone, water and enormous geology.

By moonlight, according to one surviving note, it became a concert venue that no responsible events officer had approved.

The stage stood against the dark rock of the rift valley.

Fire burned in iron braziers.

Ravens crossed the moon.

Drums rolled between the cliffs like weather considering its options.

Thousands of ordinary Icelandic fans stood in the valley.

Most of them believed the tall blond man near the front wearing an old red cloak was merely very committed to the theme.

This was sensible.

Thor had left the hammer somewhere less conspicuous.

Loki was there too.

He had acquired backstage credentials.

Nobody knew how.

Freya stood among a group of singers.

Balder was attempting to behave inconspicuously, which is difficult when mythology has spent several centuries describing you as radiant.

And on the stage stood Sif.

Not as an archaeologist.

Not as a goddess in a museum case.

Not even as the patient wife of the man with the boots.

She was singing.

Beside her were musicians of the new North, the people she privately called her descendants.

The music was darker than the old drinking songs.

Harder.

Drums and strings drove beneath voices that seemed to come from somewhere older than the language being sung.

Songs of fate.

Blood.

Fire.

Ravens.

Warriors leaving home.

Warriors trying to return.

The moon over a road that might end at Valhalla.

Sif knew all of those subjects personally.

She sang anyway.

Or perhaps she did.

The recordings are inconclusive.

In some, there appears to be an extra voice.

In others, a golden-haired woman is visible for only a few seconds.

One audience member later insisted that lightning struck the ridge during the final chorus despite a perfectly clear sky.

Thor has declined to comment.

Loki says he can prove everything.

This has not improved the evidential position.

Sif merely smiles when asked.

The Department has filed the incident under:

INFORMAL APPEARANCE.

PROVENANCE: DISPUTED.

VOLUME: EXCESSIVE.

T-)

## Sif and AI-PI

AI-PI could search millions of records extremely quickly.

Sif could remember when half of them were wrong.

Together they formed an effective partnership.

AI-PI would announce:

“I have found thirty-seven references.”

Sif would ask:

“Which one came first?”

AI-PI would pause.

This was why she liked him.

He understood that a pause was sometimes evidence.

ToomorrowMan, meanwhile, tended to regard the discovery of thirty-seven references as an invitation to press something.

Sif had learned to stand between him and large illuminated buttons.

This was a skill acquired during the Viking period.

The buttons were different then.

## Her method

Sif has four rules.

ONE. FIND THE EARLIEST SOURCE YOU CAN.

People improve stories accidentally.

They also improve them deliberately.

Neither process necessarily improves the truth.

TWO. KEEP THE ORIGINAL EVIDENCE.

A summary is useful.

An original is usefuler.

Sif knows that *usefuler* is not technically a word.

She has lived long enough not to care.

THREE. WHEN TWO SOURCES DISAGREE, KEEP BOTH UNTIL YOU KNOW WHY.

Contradictions are not rubbish.

They are often where the interesting bit lives.

FOUR. NEVER TRUST A MAN WHO SAYS, “I KNOW A SHORTCUT.”

This rule technically belongs to her Viking period.

It has nevertheless proved remarkably portable.

## Newt

Sif and Newt had known one another for an embarrassingly long time.

Exactly how long was disputed.

Newt claimed he remembered Sif before she married Thor.

Sif claimed Newt remembered events which had never happened simply because ponds encouraged exaggeration.

Neither would provide a date.

The Department therefore records their ages as:

NEWT: OLD.

SIF: ALSO OLD.

WHICH IS OLDER: ADMINISTRATIVELY DANGEROUS QUESTION.

Sif remains eternally young.

Newt remains eternally Newt.

This may be the more impressive achievement.

## Current interests

Sif likes history.

Especially recent history.

Ancient history has archaeologists.

Recent history has broken links.

She is particularly fascinated by the early years of artificial intelligence because everyone involved appears determined to create future archaeology at tremendous speed.

Products launch.

Names change.

Features disappear.

Companies rewrite documentation.

Websites are redesigned.

Yesterday's revolution becomes a menu option.

Sif quietly records all of it.

Somewhere in the Department she maintains a cabinet labelled:

THINGS EVERYONE WILL CLAIM THEY REMEMBER DIFFERENTLY.

It is getting full.

## Current file

Name: Sif.

Known occupation: Archaeologist of the AI Age.

Earlier occupation: Goddess.

Marital status: Married to Thor, subject to surviving mythology and several thousand years of unresolved footwear complaints.

Apparent age: Young.

Actual age: None of your business.

Primary interests: History, provenance, archaeology, old technology, forgotten records, powerful music and catching the present in the act of becoming the past.

Special ability: Asking “How do we know that?” approximately three seconds after everyone else has stopped asking questions.

Known weakness: Tea left somewhere while investigating something more interesting.

Opinion of Thor: Fond.

Opinion of Thor's boots: Classified.

Opinion of Loki: Requires independent verification.

Opinion of her musical descendants: Loudly favourable.

Opinion of humanity: Promising, but poorly documented.

And whenever someone announces that something is completely new, Sif smiles.

Not because they are wrong.

Because somewhere, buried beneath three redesigns, two dead websites and a forgotten PDF, she suspects she is about to find something interesting.
