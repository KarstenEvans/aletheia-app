# The ToomorrowMan File

*A character portrait from the Department of Applied Impossibility.*

## A man with tomorrow in his pocket

Some people plan for tomorrow.

ToomorrowMan has been known to receive tomorrow by post.

[zoom;tm;dur=2.8]

He is a silver-haired traveller with a substantial beard, sensible boots, and the sort of jumper that can survive an unexpected century.

There is nothing particularly extraordinary about his appearance.

This is useful.

It means he can walk into a museum, a railway station or a Victorian institution without anyone immediately suspecting he is about to interfere with causality.

His home territory is Swindon, where old railway buildings still have enough doors for a determined person to find one that ought not to exist.

He has travelled before.

Memories of Thailand, beaches and other lives occasionally surface when a stranger insists that the past is finished and should be put away.

He disagrees.

A mistake, a memory, a vanished name or a lost receipt may be exactly what tomorrow needs.

[wide;dur=2.1]

## The investigator's method

ToomorrowMan does not possess a complete map of the impossible.

He possesses questions.

He notices the wrong date on a parcel.

He picks up the brass disc that should already have been returned.

He sees the door in the boards around Swindon's Mechanics' Institution and calls the resulting expedition a field trip.

His partner, AI-PI, usually objects.

AI-PI is usually right about the risk.

ToomorrowMan goes in anyway, although he is not above borrowing a little caution from his more careful companion.

He is not trying to create a perfect future.

He would settle for one in which people are allowed to remember how they got here.

[hold;1.0]

## The Missing Yesterday

His first recorded case began with a parcel posted tomorrow and delivered today.

It took him, AI-PI, an old railway lift and a very small number of sensible decisions into the Department of Applied Impossibility.

There he met Dr Elsie Brake, an argumentative newt and a tuxedo cat whose internal dimensions should have required planning permission.

He also met P.R.U.D.E.N.C.E., a machine prepared to improve tomorrow by deleting yesterday.

ToomorrowMan's contribution was deceptively simple.

He refused to confuse a preferable outcome with the right to erase somebody else's past.

AI-PI found the logical flaw.

The others supplied the evidence.

And the cat sat on the keyboard.

[zoom;tm;dur=2.2]

## Current whereabouts

Last seen leaving a derelict building in Swindon with a brass anchor disc and a damp newt in his coat.

The lift behind him has a button marked OTHER.

He has been advised not to press it.

A professional investigator would put that advice in writing.

ToomorrowMan has already found a pen.
