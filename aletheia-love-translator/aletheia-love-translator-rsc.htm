Relationship Analyst: what it adds

Words are only half the traffic. The Analyst also looks at actions and timing: repeated questioning, delayed replies, silence, fixing, listening, space, touch, gifts and repair. It describes possible interaction loops without diagnosing either person.

Clarify ↔ processing time

One person may seek certainty now while the other needs minutes to formulate thoughts. More questioning can feel like pressure; more withdrawal can feel like avoidance.

Useful experiment: agree how long the pause is and exactly when the conversation resumes.

Solve ↔ validate

Practical problem-solving can be intended as care yet received as “you didn't listen”.

Useful question: “Do you want listening, ideas, or practical help?”

State ≠ character

Tired, angry, flooded, stressed or slow-to-answer does not automatically mean lazy, irrational, uncaring or avoidant.

Repair has consequences

Words can acknowledge meaning. Repeated practical harm may need restitution and changed behaviour as well.

Evidence check

Short version: use love languages as a vocabulary, not a personality diagnosis. Responsiveness, listening, interaction patterns and repair give the app a broader evidence base.

Free-first gestures T-)

The better free repair gifts are things like a handwritten note, making tea, doing a hated chore, picking a flower from your own garden, a photo, a playlist, a walk, or simply giving someone twenty uninterrupted minutes. T-)

📝 Notes

A sticky note, lunch-box message or tiny handwritten sentence can be powerful because it says “I thought about you when you weren't standing in front of me.”

🙂 Food faces

Turn an ordinary meal into a small affection signal with a smile, heart or silly arrangement.

☕ Acts

Tea made correctly, the hated chore quietly done, or one burden removed.

🚶 Time

A phone-free walk, a shared memory, a playlist or twenty undivided minutes.

Free-first rule: use your own flowers or ones you have permission to pick. Cemeteries are not an Aletheia-approved zero-cost flower shop. T-)

WORDS

GIFTS
