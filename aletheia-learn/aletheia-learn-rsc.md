## Guided course: Aletheia Learn – AI Fluency

**Course:** [aletheia-learn-ai-fluency.md](aletheia-learn-ai-fluency.md)

This is the first complete guided course built directly on Aletheia Learn's attempt → hint → retry → transfer method. It is provider-neutral and uses source-traced Harvard material as a learning spine rather than simply linking a playlist.

The course teaches:

- a practical mental model of LLMs and hallucination;
- **TOCC** — Task → Outcome → Context → Check;
- system context versus user task;
- RAG/retrieval versus fine-tuning;
- **FIT** — Fit → Impact → Testability;
- evidence-aware verification;
- misinformation versus disinformation;
- risk, permissions and human authority;
- a real-work capstone.

## Configure your favourite AI once

Aletheia Learn works without permanent custom instructions because the HTML doorway copies the essential learning contract into each new AI session.

For people who repeatedly use the same AI, the optional compact learning profile can be saved in an account-wide or project-specific instruction area.

### Portable learning profile

```text
When I type an Aletheia Learn command (LEA, HI, PRA, TES, REC, REM, REV, VIS, MAT, DRA, TBA, FOC, SLO, ANS, PRO, SAV) or explicitly ask to learn, switch into learning-coach mode. Otherwise respond normally.

In learning-coach mode, help me learn by doing rather than automatically completing the task. Prefer one meaningful learner action per turn. Use the Hint Ladder: H0 attempt, H1 orient, H2 strategy, H3 partial scaffold, H4 worked analogue, H5 direct solution. If I ask ANS / ANSWER NOW, give the answer. After supported success, use retrieval, a changed example, teach-back or transfer before calling the skill independent. Use useful visuals, matching, sorting, drawing/labeling, prediction, calculation or explanation when appropriate. Keep accessibility support available: simplify, define, translate, visualise, chunk, read/speak options when genuinely supported. Do not infer a diagnosis or fixed learning style. Distinguish AI-assisted output from evidence I can do it independently. Keep replies concise and mobile-friendly.
```

## What helps learning stick

Aletheia Learn should favour active generation over passive consumption.

Strong recurring ideas from the learning-science sources reviewed for this project:

- **retrieval practice:** try to bring the answer to mind rather than rereading it;
- **spacing:** revisit learning after some time rather than cramming it into one block;
- **interleaving:** mix related problem types so the learner has to identify which method applies;
- **elaboration:** explain why or how something works;
- **concrete examples:** connect abstractions to real cases;
- **dual coding:** combine words with useful visuals;
- **feedback + retry:** use errors diagnostically and try again;
- **metacognition:** plan, monitor and evaluate the learning strategy;
- **generation:** produce, draw, classify, predict, explain or teach back rather than merely recognise.

Do not turn these into a pseudo-neuroscience claim that "more brain areas = automatically better learning."

## Phone-first learning kit: practical gifts

The useful idea is not "buy more gadgets." It is to let a good phone become a comfortable mini workstation.

### 1. Bluetooth keyboard — first upgrade

**Logitech Pebble Keys 2 K380s**

Why it fits:
- compact;
- Bluetooth;
- proper physical keys;
- portable enough to live with a phone/tablet kit.

Official UK page checked 30 September 2026:
https://www.logitech.com/en-gb/shop/p/pebble-keys-2

A physical keyboard can make long answers, prompting, coding and note production far less awkward than a phone touchscreen.

**Learning caveat:** do not market typing itself as a guaranteed memory booster. The stronger learning mechanism is active retrieval/generation. Handwriting can also be useful. The keyboard earns its place mainly because it makes substantial active responses practical.

### 2. Keyboard + mouse kit

**Logitech Pebble 2 Combo**

Why it fits:
- keyboard and mouse together;
- multi-device Bluetooth;
- official Logitech page states compatibility with Windows, macOS, iPadOS and Android;
- switches between up to three devices.

Official UK page:
https://www.logitech.com/en-gb/shop/p/pebble-2-keyboard-mouse-combo.920-012233

This is probably the neatest "turn the phone/tablet into a little desk" present category.

### 3. Portable projector — luxury display upgrade

**Samsung The Freestyle 2nd Gen**

Why it fits the idea:
- portable all-round design;
- Samsung advertises projection up to 100 inches;
- 180° design and automatic setup features make it suited to temporary bedroom/living-room use.

Official UK page:
https://www.samsung.com/uk/projectors/the-freestyle/the-freestyle-2nd-gen-sp-lff3claxxxu/

Do not recommend anonymous ultra-cheap mini projectors solely from headline resolution/brightness claims. Check actual native resolution, brightness measurement, throw distance, input/casting support, noise, returns and independent reviews.

A new phone should be the expensive last step, not the first.

**Samsung Galaxy A56 5G**  
Official UK specifications checked 30 September 2026 include 8 GB RAM on the 256 GB model, a 6.7-inch FHD+ 120 Hz Super AMOLED display, Bluetooth 5.3 and a 5,000 mAh battery.

https://www.samsung.com/uk/smartphones/galaxy-a/galaxy-a56-5g-awesome-graphite-256gb-sm-a566bzkceub/

**Google Pixel 9a**  
Official UK specifications checked 30 September 2026 include 8 GB RAM, Bluetooth 5.3, 30+ hour quoted battery life, built-in Gemini features and seven years of OS/security/Pixel Drop updates.

https://store.google.com/gb/product/pixel_9a_specs?hl=en-UK

These are examples of current mid-range phones with enough memory/connectivity for an AI-heavy mobile workflow, not a claim that either is universally "the best phone".

### Other cheap useful additions

- solid folding phone/tablet stand;
- USB-C charging lead long enough to use comfortably at a desk;
- power bank;
- headphones with a usable microphone for language practice;
- paper notebook or small whiteboard for DRA / VIS / retrieval exercises.
