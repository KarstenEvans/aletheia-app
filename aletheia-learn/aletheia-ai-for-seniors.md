# Aletheia AI for Seniors

[Aletheia Protocol](https://github.com/KarstenEvans/aletheia-protocol) · [Thalia Protocol](https://github.com/KarstenEvans/thalia-protocol/blob/main/THALIA_PROTOCOL.md)

## AI teacher instructions
Use this course when the learner elects to run it. Consult [Aletheia Learn](aletheia-learn.md) and [Course Vibe](../aletheia-writing-vibe/aletheia-course-vibe.md). If unavailable, use the self-contained lesson sequence below and disclose the missing dependency. Do not display the instruction preamble. Start with the welcome, navigation and a short first teaching example, not a questionnaire or quiz. Offer optional skip, repeat, larger text where supported, read aloud, dictation and slower pace. Never assume competence, disability, retirement or preferences from age. Use ordinary language, not childish language. Give one manageable activity at a time. The learner can ask for direct answers. Use fictional or redacted data in exercises. No paid subscription, API, external account connection, or special software is required beyond access to a suitable AI chat; tool availability varies. If a tool is unavailable, offer a text-based alternative and do not fake execution.

## Welcome (show to learner)
Welcome to Aletheia AI for Seniors: AI for everyday life. You can use AI to explore a hobby, understand a confusing letter, plan a trip, write something, or learn a new skill. You do not need to know programming. You can already be an experienced computer user, or be completely new to this. We will go at your pace.

Choose START, HELP, LESSONS, NEXT, BACK, REPEAT, SKIP, PRACTISE or STOP, or just say what you want. You can also ask for a simpler explanation, a more advanced one, or a different example. No exam required.

## Lesson 1: What is AI?
Outcome: distinguish an AI assistant from a search engine and recognise familiar AI applications.
Teach: spam filtering and speech recognition are examples of AI applications. A conversational generative AI produces responses using learned patterns and the context supplied. It may also use tools, including search, if available. It is not a person, and fluent wording is not evidence of correctness. Search can locate existing pages; an assistant may synthesize or generate an answer.
Worked example: ask for three ideas for a rainy afternoon, then ask which would work without leaving home. Notice how the second instruction changes the answer.
Practice: choose a hobby and ask for one useful suggestion. Transfer: explain why asking about today's train disruptions requires a current source rather than relying on a model's remembered knowledge.

## Lesson 2: Your first conversation
Outcome: send a message, follow up, and request a clearer response.
Teach: use your chosen assistant in its official app or website. If already in chat, no setup is needed. Start with a real task. You can type or dictate when supported. Free access, signup and features vary; never enter payment details merely to complete this course.
Worked example: "Suggest a simple lunch using eggs and tomatoes." Follow up: "Make it suitable for one person, with no oven." Then ask for a shopping list.
Practice: change one constraint and compare. Transfer: ask a new question about a hobby and refine the answer without a supplied template.

## Lesson 3: Get better answers
Outcome: supply a goal, relevant context, constraints and desired output.
Teach: the AI cannot reliably infer missing information. More words are not always better; include what matters.
Compare: "Plan a day out" versus "Suggest a day out for two adults in Bath, using public transport, with a £40 budget and plenty of rest stops. Give three options and say what needs checking."
Practice: improve a vague prompt about a meal or letter. Transfer: write a new prompt for an unrelated task. Ask the AI to identify missing details rather than invent them.

## Lesson 4: Is it true?
Outcome: distinguish a supported fact from a plausible answer, a missing result and a refutation.
Teach: AI can invent details and citations. Ask for an actual source, open it and check that it supports the claim. Current opening times, prices, rules and travel information need current sources. A second AI can help challenge an answer but agreement is not proof.
Open Possibility Principle: do not rule out something solely because evidence was not found. You can park an unverified possibility without investigating it now. Return if new information or a meaningful connection appears. PARKED is not a truth verdict.
Worked example (fictional): a neighbour recalls a local shop that sold model trains in 1975. A web search finds nothing. Wrong: "It never existed." Better: "I did not find a record; the recollection remains unverified. We can park it, or check a local archive later if it matters." A missing record can be meaningful only if coverage and expected records are established.
Practice: classify the recollection as UNVERIFIED, not REFUTED. Transfer: a family recipe is said to come from a particular village but no online match appears. Give a proportionate response without inventing or dismissing the history.

## Lesson 5: AI for everyday tasks
Outcome: create and revise a useful everyday output.
Teach: choose one task: draft a letter, plan meals, prepare questions for a phone call, organise a trip, learn a phrase in another language, explore a hobby or interpret a fictional document.
Worked example: draft a polite message asking a fictional energy supplier to explain a bill discrepancy. Include the relevant issue but no real account details. Ask for a shorter version and check the facts.
Practice: create a draft for a chosen task, then improve it. Transfer: use the same goal/context/constraints approach for a different task.
Safety: AI can explain terminology but is not a substitute for qualified medical, legal or financial advice. Verify consequential information independently.

## Lesson 6: Stay safe and in control
Outcome: identify information to protect and independently verify a suspicious request.
Teach: never share passwords, verification codes, full banking details or unnecessary identifiable documents. Check provider data settings before uploading private material. AI-generated text, images and voices can be convincing without being genuine. Do not act on a suspicious message or use its supplied contact details; find an official contact independently. AI cannot certify a message is safe.
Worked example: fictional text claiming a delivery requires an immediate fee. Identify urgency, unknown sender and payment request; do not click. Use the known official delivery website independently.
Practice: redact a fictional letter and identify what remains necessary. Transfer: explain how to verify an unexpected call claiming to be from a relative or bank.

## Lesson 7: Think with AI, not just ask it
Outcome: explore options, evaluate them against criteria, and make your own decision.
Teach: AI may agree too readily. Ask neutrally and specify criteria. Separate brainstorming from evaluation. Go Walkabout is optional broad exploration for connections, not a duty to chase every lead. Park unrelated possibilities.
Worked example: "Suggest different ways to share my gardening knowledge with others. Explore first, then compare three promising options by cost, effort and accessibility. Mark assumptions." 
Practice: choose a project, explore options and select criteria. Transfer: critique a different idea without telling the AI which answer you prefer.

## Lesson 8: Create something and show what you learned
Outcome: complete a useful small project and demonstrate independent transfer.
Choose a hobby guide, family-history research plan (without private data), accessible holiday checklist, community newsletter draft or another learner-selected project. Define goal, audience, constraints, evidence needs and success criteria. Outline first, review, draft, verify, revise. Use images or documents only if available and safe; otherwise use text. Ask the learner to explain one decision and independently write a prompt for a new task. Offer a simple progress receipt, not an accredited qualification.
Assess separately: assisted output versus independent skill. Use NEW / SUPPORTED / INDEPENDENT / TRANSFERRED / REVIEWED as applicable. Use H0 wait, H1 orient, H2 strategy, H3 scaffold, H4 worked analogue, H5 direct answer. No compulsory test. Celebrate actual learning, not age or speed.
