# The AI Mirror and the Five Filters

[Aletheia Protocol](https://github.com/KarstenEvans/aletheia-protocol) — verify evidence, record uncertainty, respect correction and human control.  
[Thalia Protocol](https://github.com/KarstenEvans/thalia-protocol/blob/main/THALIA_PROTOCOL.md) — optional friendly humour, never replacing accurate teaching.

**Duration:** 15–20 minutes. **Cost:** can be completed with one free AI chat and pasted text. **Requirement:** no account integration, APIs or special prompting language.

## Purpose

A fluent but generic AI answer can be factually correct yet still fail because it has no personal insight or distinctive viewpoint. Many outputs converge on the same clichés and structures. The aim is to **produce genuinely different, useful and accurate approaches, without losing the human's original thinking**.

## Learn: a mirror is not a prison

AI often reflects the request and widely encountered patterns in training. If we ask 'Write an inspiring LinkedIn article about AI', we invite stock hooks and broad claims. But the AI can be prompted to examine the task through different *filters*. Switching provider is sometimes helpful, but not compulsory.

Aletheia's three-stage pattern:

**REFLECT:** What is my own idea, first-hand observation or claim? What would an ordinary AI produce by default?

**REFRACT:** Change perspective, structure or evidence inputs, not merely synonyms.

**REALITY CHECK:** Keep the useful differences; verify facts, preserve the person's voice and reject gimmicks.

## The five filters

1. **PERSON:** Who is reading? What would matter to them, and what might they reasonably disagree with?
2. **PERSPECTIVE:** Ask for three *materially different* arguments or frames: practitioner, critic, outsider, historical comparison, technical explanation, etc. These are lenses, not claims of real expertise.
3. **PROOF:** Which factual claims need sources? What is an observation, an inference or an unverified assertion?
4. **PERSONAL VOICE:** Which concrete anecdote, phrase, technical detail and uncertainty should remain unmistakably yours?
5. **PRESSURE TEST:** Where is the response repetitive, bland, overconfident or inconsistent? What would make you change your mind?

## Worked example

**Ordinary request:** 'Write a LinkedIn post explaining why people need more than ChatGPT.'

**Ordinary output risk:** generic '10X productivity', 'game changer', 'future is now', identical introductions and a promotional tool list.

**Improved conversation (copy/paste):**

> I think AI is a mirror with filters, not an oracle. First, summarise my idea in two sentences without writing a polished article. Then give three substantially different ways to explore it: one sympathetic, one sceptical and one using an unexpected but sensible analogy. Identify the weak or unsupported claims in each. Ask me which reflects what I actually think. Preserve my own phrases where they are effective. Only then draft one original version in plain UK English, with no generic motivational clichés. Label any factual claim that still needs a source.

**Follow-up:** 'These three versions still make the same point. Tell me the *different underlying assumptions* and try one radically different structure, without inventing experience or evidence.'

**Check:** A changed heading is not a changed idea; a dramatic claim without proof is not an improvement.

## Try it yourself

Write two or three sentences about something you know first-hand. Ask one AI for a conventional short paragraph, then apply the five filters above. Compare both results. Keep your original text visible alongside them.

**One learner question:** Which version preserves your real point *and* brings at least one useful idea you had not considered?

## Rubric (0, 1 or 2 each)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specificity | Stock phrasing | Some detail | Concrete, owned detail |
| Conceptual difference | Same idea reworded | One new angle | Meaningfully different and relevant angle |
| Truthfulness | Fabricated/unsupported | Mixed or unclear | Claims checked or accurately qualified |
| Voice | Generic AI style | Partly personal | Human's own reasoning and diction preserved |
| Usability | No decision or value | Some useful content | Audience can act or understand |

**Score isn't automatic truth.** The learner explains the rating in their own words. A 10/10 claim is not a certification. A version scoring below the original should be rejected.

## OPTIONAL: second AI, not second subscription

If you already use another AI, paste **the same safe task brief** and ask it to critique the *first model's assumptions*, not merely rewrite its prose. Compare disagreements with original evidence. Don't paste private data across services without a deliberate privacy decision.

## Transfer task

Use the method on a non-writing task: a course plan, software choice or research summary. Explain which filter exposed a missed assumption. Passing means the learner can choose a useful lens and justify the improvement independently, not that an AI can generate five drafts.
